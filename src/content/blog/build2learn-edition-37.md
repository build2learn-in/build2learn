_Build2Learn at Grootan: Today's Build2Learn was an absolute blast._

First things first — a huge shoutout to **Grootan Technologies** for hosting us. Easily one of the best venues we've had: no trouble finding the place, no Wi-Fi drama, no networking issues. When the logistics just work, the whole event runs on a different energy, and today was proof of that. Thank you, Grootan!

People started trickling in a good thirty minutes early (a Build2Learn tradition at this point), and by the time introductions wrapped up, teams had already formed. Ideas were bouncing off each other before anyone had even opened a laptop. Here's what everyone built.

## The Projects That Came to Life

---

### Memories, Pinned to Places — by Rikshita & Sujitha

<figure style="text-align: center; margin: 1.5rem 0;">
  <img src="/images/blog/build2learn_edition37_memories.png" alt="Memories, Pinned to Places by Rikshita and Sujitha" style="max-width: 100%; border-radius: 12px;" />
</figure>

Our first demo was a web app built around a simple, lovely idea: every place holds a memory. You save a memory — your voice, your emotion, that moment — to a specific location. Later, you can pull up a map of your city and travel back to exactly what you were feeling at that spot. A map of places that's really a map of moments.

---

### A Local On-Device AI Journal — by Vignesh, Yogesh & Bharath

<figure style="text-align: center; margin: 1.5rem 0;">
  <img src="/images/blog/build2learn_edition37_ai_journal.png" alt="AI Journal by Vignesh, Yogesh and Bharath" style="max-width: 100%; border-radius: 12px;" />
</figure>

Next up, a journal that lives on your laptop, with an AI agent that chimes in as you write. Look back at how your mood moved through the week or the month, and get insights on your patterns. A lot of potential here — waiting to see how this one materialises.

### Phone Scrabble — by Ashish (yes, me)

<figure style="text-align: center; margin: 1.5rem 0;">
  <img src="/images/blog/build2learn_edition37_phone_scrabble.png" alt="Phone Scrabble by Ashish" style="max-width: 100%; border-radius: 12px;" />
</figure>

I built a game that's basically Scrabble, except the tiles are your phones. Everyone at the meetup got randomly assigned to a team of five, each with its own colour, and the whole room played together. I don't have a photo of the chaos, but the [video here](https://photos.app.goo.gl/BRJk7n9jc1HwiLbBA) should give you a feel for how it played out.

---

### Prompt-to-Website, No AI — by Melvin & Anand

<figure style="text-align: center; margin: 1.5rem 0;">
  <img src="/images/blog/build2learn_edition37_prompt_to_website.png" alt="Prompt-to-Website by Melvin and Anand" style="max-width: 100%; border-radius: 12px;" />
</figure>

Melvin and Anand built a tool that converts your text prompt into actual UI components for static sites — think of it like a Jekyll-style framework for coding on the go, and there's no AI involved. It's built on Bootstrap, which honestly brought back my PHP days of stitching components together from all over the internet. Full 2015–2018 vibes, and I mean that as a compliment. Oh, and it ships as a VS Code extension. How amazing is that?

---

### AI Lead Generator — by Sai, Balaji & Harsini

<figure style="text-align: center; margin: 1.5rem 0;">
  <img src="/images/blog/build2learn_edition37_ai_lead_generator.png" alt="AI Lead Generator by Sai, Balaji and Harsini" style="max-width: 100%; border-radius: 12px;" />
</figure>

A team of college friends built a tool that generates leads for your product. Point it at your website, and it learns what you're building, scrapes Reddit, Twitter and beyond, finds people talking about the exact pain point you solve, and hands you a list of people to reach out to. Prospecting on autopilot.

---

### The Digital Wardrobe — by Ram & Suresh

<figure style="text-align: center; margin: 1.5rem 0;">
  <img src="/images/blog/build2learn_edition37_digital_wardrobe.png" alt="The Digital Wardrobe by Ram and Suresh" style="max-width: 100%; border-radius: 12px;" />
</figure>

Two people, one idea, two very different origin stories. Ram built it because his wife wanted it. Suresh built it because his friends keep telling him, "You have only one shirt."

The idea is simple: a digital wardrobe. Click, scan, and every piece of clothing you own gets stored. Want to find that one pant, or check if you have anything in a particular colour? Just search — no more digging through the mountain of mess that is your actual wardrobe.

### Tamil EBook Reader — by Suresh, Ragul, Rakesh & Gokul

<figure style="text-align: center; margin: 1.5rem 0;">
  <img src="/images/blog/build2learn_edition37_tamil_ebook.png" alt="Tamil EBook Reader by Suresh, Ragul, Rakesh and Gokul" style="max-width: 100%; border-radius: 12px;" />

### Classifying Bharatanatyam Mudras — by Brahadeesh & Joshua

<figure style="text-align: center; margin: 1.5rem 0;">
  <img src="/images/blog/build2learn_edition37_mudras.png" alt="Classifying Bharatanatyam Mudras by Brahadeesh and Joshua" style="max-width: 100%; border-radius: 12px;" />
</figure>

Then came the Mudra team. Brahadeesh — famous around here for not touching AI tools at all — teamed up with Joshua to map and classify the hand Mudras used in Bharatanatyam. Building your own classifier from scratch is a painstaking process, and Brahadeesh got five Mudras working in the span of two hours. Loved it.

---

### 0 Accountant — by Mani

<figure style="text-align: center; margin: 1.5rem 0;">
  <img src="/images/blog/build2learn_edition37_zero_accountant.png" alt="0 Accountant by Mani" style="max-width: 100%; border-radius: 12px;" />
</figure>

Mani did what Mani does: built an app that looks absolutely gorgeous. His UI skills are top of the line. 0 Accountant turns your bills into clean records — upload with a click, and an internal pipeline processes each bill and stores it in a database. Need to query your expenses later? One click.

---

### Rural Health Scheduler — by Nasik, Sasi & Sandeep

<figure style="text-align: center; margin: 1.5rem 0;">
  <img src="/images/blog/build2learn_edition37_rural_health.png" alt="Rural Health Scheduler by Nasik, Sasi and Sandeep" style="max-width: 100%; border-radius: 12px;" />
</figure>

This one comes from a real need: an NGO trying to bridge the gap between rural areas that need medical volunteers and doctors who have free time. The team built a working prototype that integrates with Google Calendar, so doctors can plug volunteering slots straight into their existing schedules. Nice work, guys — this is the kind of project I love seeing at Build2Learn.

### Fridge-to-Food Recipe Improviser — by Harshini & Sajad

<figure style="text-align: center; margin: 1.5rem 0;">
  <img src="/images/blog/build2learn_edition37_fridge_to_food.png" alt="Fridge-to-Food Recipe Improviser by Harshini and Sajad" style="max-width: 100%; border-radius: 12px;" />
</figure>

Tell it what's sitting in your fridge, and it tells you what you can actually cook with it. Dinner, solved.

### The Doomscroll Alarm — by Syed, Aatif, Hari & Yuvaraj

<figure style="text-align: center; margin: 1.5rem 0;">
  <img src="/images/blog/build2learn_edition37_doomscroll.png" alt="The Doomscroll Alarm by Syed, Aatif, Hari and Yuvaraj" style="max-width: 100%; border-radius: 12px;" />
</figure>

Last but definitely not least: an app that catches you doomscrolling. Your camera watches, a classifier detects the telltale scroll, and the moment you slip into the void — a loud meme blasts onto your screen to snap you out of it. They hit some TensorFlow/OpenCV bumps along the way, but still managed to demo the risk detection live. Public shaming as a productivity tool. I respect it.
